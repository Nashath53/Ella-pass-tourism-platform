History & culture

The story of Ella

From Ravana's legend to British tea estates, a colonial railway and a modern cyclone — the history behind the places you are visiting.

Five things to know

  1. The name "Ella" is believed to come from the Sinhala word for waterfall.
  2. The Nine Arch Bridge was built around 1919–1921 without structural steel.
  3. Tea replaced coffee after a leaf-rust epidemic destroyed the estates in the 1860s.
  4. The Demodara Loop is the only railway loop in Sri Lanka.
  5. The Kandy–Ella through train is closed since Cyclone Ditwah; Nanu Oya–Ella–Badulla reopened 20 June 2026.

How we write history

Facts, legends and CHECK labels

Where two reliable sources agree, we write the fact plainly. Where a story is local tradition without documentary evidence, we say so. Where a price or opening day changes often, we add a Check before you go label. Nothing on this page is invented.

A timeline of Ella and the hills

  • Legend
    The Ravana tradition Legend, not recorded history

    In the Ramayana tradition, King Ravana hid Princess Sita in caves around Ella, which is why Ravana Falls and Ravana Cave carry his name. This is legend, not recorded history. The places are real; the story is a tradition that has shaped the region's identity for centuries.

  • 307–266 BC
    Muthiyangana, Badulla

    Muthiyangana in Badulla is believed to have been visited by the Buddha and is one of the 16 sacred places (Solosmasthana). King Devanampiya Tissa developed it into a temple complex. The temple is still active and welcomes visitors; dress modestly and arrive between 7–9 AM or 4–6 PM.

  • 1st c. BC
    Dowa Rock Temple

    Believed to have been built by King Valagamba while he took refuge in Uva after a South Indian invasion. An 11-metre unfinished Buddha is carved directly into the rock face. It has been a protected archaeological monument since 1996.

  • c. 10th c.
    Buduruwagala rock carvings, near Wellawaya

    Seven Buddhist figures carved at Buduruwagala near Wellawaya, including a rare standing Maitreya figure with a flame-shaped headdress. About 40 km south of Ella.

  • 1815–1818
    The British period and the Uva–Wellassa uprising

    The British took the Kandyan Kingdom in 1815. The Uva–Wellassa uprising of 1817–1818 was crushed with villages burned and many people killed across the hill country.

  • 1800s
    Coffee, then tea — and the plantation workers

    The hills were cleared for coffee. Leaf rust destroyed the coffee estates from the 1860s and planters switched to tea. Workers brought from southern India became the Hill Country Tamil communities who still live and work on the estates today. Their culture and language are part of the hill country's identity.

  • 1867
    The Colombo–Kandy railway

    The first railway in Ceylon opened between Colombo and Kandy to carry coffee out of the hills. The line to Badulla would take another 57 years to complete.

  • 1890
    Thomas Lipton buys Dambatenne

    The Scottish merchant Thomas Lipton bought the Dambatenne estate near Haputale. The viewpoint on the hill above it is named Lipton's Seat. He would eventually build the tea brand that carried his name around the world.

  • c. 1919–1921
    Nine Arch Bridge completed Heritage landmark

    About 91 m long and 24 m high, built of brick, stone and cement without structural steel. Sources give completion dates ranging from 1919 to 1921. A local story credits builder P.K. Appuhami; one heritage source names Harold Cuthbert Marwood of the Ceylon Government Railway as designer. The popular claim that steel was diverted to World War I is a local story without confirmed documentary evidence.

  • Early 1920s
    Demodara Loop

    The line circles a hill and passes through Tunnel No. 42 directly beneath Demodara station to maintain the 1-in-44 gradient required to descend into the valley. Credited to engineer D.J. Wimalasurendra. A local legend says a farmer's turban blowing in the wind gave him the idea for the loop — it is a tradition, not a documented fact.

  • 5 Feb 1924
    The line reaches Badulla

    The final section of the hill-country line to Badulla opened, completing one of the engineering achievements of colonial Ceylon. Today's trains still run the same route.

  • 1927–1931
    Adisham Hall built near Haputale

    Sir Thomas Villiers, a planter and trading-company chairman, built Adisham Hall in Tudor and Jacobean style. It has been a Benedictine monastery since the 1960s. Open on Saturdays, Sundays, poya days and public holidays.

  • 2014
    Commemorative stamps

    Sri Lanka issued commemorative stamps for the Demodara Loop and the Nine Arch Bridge, recognising them as national heritage landmarks.

  • 2025–2026
    Cyclone Ditwah and the railway closure Still open for this section

    Cyclone Ditwah damaged the hill-country line. The Nanu Oya–Ella–Badulla section reopened on 20 June 2026. The Kandy–Ella through service remains closed. Full reopening is expected around early 2027, but no confirmed date has been given.

The stories behind the places

Nine Arch Bridge — "Bridge in the Sky"

One heritage source names Harold Cuthbert Marwood of the Ceylon Government Railway as the designer. A local story credits builder P.K. Appuhami, said to have filled the valley with boulders. Historians note there is no documented evidence for this account, so it is presented here as tradition. What is certain: the bridge was completed without structural steel, a remarkable achievement for its era.

Demodara Loop — the turban legend Legend

Engineer D.J. Wimalasurendra is credited with the loop. Local legend says a farmer's turban blowing in circles in the wind gave him the idea. The engineering solution is real; the legend is a story. The train passes through Tunnel No. 42 directly beneath the station — an experience worth riding for yourself.

Dowa Rock Temple

The 11-metre unfinished figure has stood in the rock for over two thousand years. The carving was never completed — legend says Valagamba was called away. It is one of the few unfinished rock-cut figures in Sri Lanka and carries that atmosphere of interrupted time.

Adisham Hall

Tudor and Jacobean-style in the Sri Lankan hills — built by Sir Thomas Villiers, who chose the design because it reminded him of his English home. The Benedictine monks who have lived here since the 1960s make and sell jam from the estate's fruits; it is available in the small shop.

The railway itself

Built in stages from the 1870s, the line from Peradeniya to Badulla took over 50 years to complete. It climbs from sea level to nearly 1,900 metres, passing through 46 tunnels and over 93 bridges. The BBC described the Kandy–Badulla route as one of the world's most beautiful train journeys.
